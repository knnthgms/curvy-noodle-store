Here's the thing about buying a notebook: it feels like a small, simple decision — until you're standing in the stationery aisle (or scrolling endlessly online) trying to figure out whether you want dot grid or ruled, A5 or A6, 80gsm or 100gsm. The truth is, the "right" journal isn't the prettiest one or the most expensive one. It's the one that actually fits how you plan to use it.

This guide breaks down the three things that matter most — size, paper type, and the accessories that complete your setup — so you can pick with confidence and stay within your budget.

![a flat lay of different notebook sizes and paper types](/images/blog/different-notebook-sizes.PNG)

## Size Matters: From Pocket A6 to Spacious A4

Size is the first thing to sort out, because it decides how (and where) you'll actually use your journal. Bigger isn't better — the right size is the one you'll carry and open every day.

- **A6 (pocket-sized):** Small enough to slip into a bag or back pocket. Ideal if you want to jot notes on the go, keep a minimalist planner, or carry your journal everywhere without it weighing you down. Great for students in lectures and anyone who journals in short bursts.
- **A5 (the sweet spot):** The most popular size for a reason — roomy enough to write and lay out a week, portable enough to travel with. If you're a bullet journal beginner or a planner addict, this is usually where to start.
- **B5 (extra writing room):** A step up from A5 with noticeably more space per page. A favourite for students taking detailed notes and anyone who writes a lot by hand.
- **A4 (desk-based and spacious):** Best when your journal lives on your desk. All that room makes it perfect for sketching, project planning, and spreads where you don't want to feel boxed in.

If you're not sure, A5 is the safest first buy — it's the "does a bit of everything" size.

## Paper Type Breakdown: Dot Grid vs Ruled vs Graph

Paper is where a lot of people go wrong, because they pick by look rather than by use. Here's what each layout is genuinely best at:

- **Dot grid:** The bullet journaler's favourite. The dots give you structure to keep things aligned, but they fade into the background so your spreads don't look busy. Best for bullet journaling, custom planners, and anyone who wants flexibility without a rigid grid.
- **Ruled (lined):** The classic. Nothing beats it for plain writing — diary entries, class notes, long-form journaling. Best for students and writers who mostly put words on the page.
- **Square / graph:** The most structured option. The grid keeps everything precise, which makes it ideal for maths, diagrams, tables, and to-scale sketches.
- **Blank:** Total freedom. Best for artists, doodlers, and anyone who wants nothing on the page telling them where to draw.

One budget tip that saves regret later: check the paper weight (GSM). Lighter paper (around 70–80gsm) is cheaper but can show ghosting or bleed with markers and fountain pens. If you plan to use brush pens or highlighters, spending a little more on 100gsm+ paper is worth it.

## The Perfect Accompaniments: Pens, Stickers & Washi Tape

A journal is only half the setup. The right supplies are what turn a blank notebook into something you actually enjoy using every day.

- **Pens:** Start with a good fineliner for everyday writing, and add a brush pen or two if you like headers and lettering. Match your pens to your paper — heavier ink loves heavier paper.
- **Stickers:** The fastest way to beat the "blank page" problem. Activity and emotion stickers let you set up a spread in seconds instead of drawing every icon by hand, and habit-tracker sheets give you a satisfying visual record to keep your streak going. (More on our range below.)
- **Washi tape:** Endlessly useful — section off your week, add colour to plain pages, or tape in tickets and mementos. A couple of rolls goes a long way.
- **The extras:** Page markers, a bookmark, and a simple ruler or stencil make daily setup quicker and neater.

You don't need all of this at once. Start with a pen and a sticker sheet, and build your kit as you figure out your style.

## Matching Your Journal to Your Style

If you want a quick shortcut, here's where most people land:

- **The student:** B5 or A5, ruled, on the more affordable end. Prioritise page count and durability over decoration.
- **The bullet journal beginner:** A5 dot grid, 100gsm if your budget allows. The flexible layout grows with you.
- **The planner addict:** A5 or A6, paired with a solid sticker collection to speed up your weekly setup.
- **The notebook-obsessed (hello):** Honestly? One of each. You already know.
